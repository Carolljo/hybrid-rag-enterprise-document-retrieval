INCIDENT RESPONSE POLICY

Purpose

This policy defines how employees must identify, report, and respond to suspected information-security incidents.

1. Security Incidents

A security incident includes any event that may compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of company systems or information.

Examples include unauthorized access, malware infections, credential theft, lost devices, phishing attempts, and accidental disclosure of sensitive information.

2. Employee Reporting

Employees must report suspected security incidents promptly to the IT security team.

Employees should provide relevant information about the incident, including the affected system, approximate time, observed activity, and any actions already taken.

Employees must not intentionally conceal or delay reporting a suspected security incident.

3. Lost or Stolen Devices

Lost or stolen company laptops, phones, security tokens, or other devices must be reported immediately.

Employees should not attempt to remotely access or recover a stolen device unless instructed by authorized personnel.

The IT security team may remotely lock or erase company-managed devices when necessary to protect company information.

4. Credential Compromise

Employees who suspect that a password, authentication token, or other credential has been compromised must report the incident immediately.

Affected credentials must be changed or revoked as directed by the IT security team.

Unexpected multi-factor authentication requests may indicate attempted unauthorized access and must be denied and reported.

5. Malware and Suspicious Activity

Employees who suspect malware infection must disconnect the affected device from company networks when it is safe to do so and contact the IT security team.

Employees must not disable endpoint-security tools or attempt to remove sophisticated malware without authorization.

Suspicious login activity, unusual system behavior, and unexpected security alerts must also be reported.

6. Sensitive Information Exposure

Accidental disclosure or unauthorized exposure of confidential or restricted information must be reported promptly.

Employees should provide details about what information was exposed, how the exposure occurred, and who may have received or accessed the information.

7. Incident Investigation

The IT security team is responsible for coordinating investigation, containment, remediation, and recovery activities.

Employees must cooperate with authorized investigations and preserve relevant information when instructed.

8. Communication

Employees must not publicly discuss security incidents or communicate incident details to unauthorized external parties.

External notifications must be handled by authorized company representatives according to applicable legal and business requirements.

9. Recovery

Systems affected by a security incident may only be returned to normal operation after appropriate security checks and authorization.

Credentials, access permissions, or devices may be reset or replaced as part of recovery.

10. Post-Incident Review

Significant incidents should be reviewed after resolution to identify causes, lessons learned, and opportunities to improve security controls.

11. Compliance

Failure to report or appropriately respond to a security incident may result in removal of system access and further action under applicable company policies.
